# AM2 Programme Plan — Post-Submission Discussion Prep
**Phase 2 of the StreamWave EPA Prep · Data Technician Level 3 · ST0795**
**Revised: 13 May 2026**

This plan sets out the second half of the programme. Phase 1 (Days 0–9) prepared learners for AM1 — Scenario Demonstrations with Questioning. Phase 2 prepares them for AM2 — Professional Discussion underpinned by a Portfolio.

---

## 1. The crucial framing

**This programme begins *after* the portfolio is submitted.** Every KSB chapter is already written. The assessor already has the portfolio and the KSB mapping (the document structure *is* the mapping — each chapter is a KSB).

The job from Day 10 onwards is therefore **not** to write, revise, or add to the portfolio. It is:

1. **Find the strongest evidence inside the portfolio that's already been submitted** — re-read it strategically, mark the workplace stories that will defend well in conversation.
2. **Convert those stories into strong, defensible spoken answers** — using **STARR** as the universal answer shape (Situation · Task · Action · Result · Reflection).
3. **Add the Distinction layer** where it counts — the *evaluate / critically evaluate / justify* depth that turns Pass-level answers into Distinction-level ones. (Live evidence from real assessor feedback: apprentices repeatedly hit Pass on every KSB but fall short of Distinction depth on D1/D2/D3 — a depth gap, not a content gap.)
4. **Reduce anxiety throughout** — the writing is done; the rehearsal starts now.

The Gold Rule from Phase 1 — *minimise anxiety, maximise the ability to show off* — stays exactly. But "showing off" in AM2 is *defending a spoken answer that anchors to your already-submitted evidence*. Not building anything new.

---

## 2. What the assessment looks like

From the EPA assessment plan (ST0795) and from observed real-world feedback reports:

- **Format:** 60-minute one-to-one professional discussion (+10% assessor discretion). Audio-recorded for moderation. Held remotely via video conferencing.
- **Inputs the assessor already has:** the portfolio (typically 80–100 pages of structured Word/PDF, organised by KSB) and the KSB mapping (the chapter structure itself).
- **Questions:** minimum 10 open questions from an EPAO bank + assessor-generated. Holistic — apprentice expected to combine KSBs in answers.
- **Grades:** Fail / Pass / Distinction. Distinction descriptors (D1, D2, D3) are layered *on top of* Pass — Pass first, then Distinction depth on top.
- **Mandatory evidence flags:** several KSBs (K10, K11, K12, K13, K14, S10, S11, S12, S13, B1, B5, B6) carry a *"Mandatory evidence: Present"* requirement — specific points must be hit.

**The four content areas the discussion covers:**

1. **Data Gathering**
2. **Data Analysis and Validation**
3. **Distribution and Dissemination**
4. **Approach to Work**

**The three Distinction descriptors (D1, D2, D3):**

- **D1 — Data set use & impact of incorrect gathering** *(verb: evaluate and justify)*
- **D2 — Why data is communicated, why different methods** *(verb: critically evaluate)*
- **D3 — Storage / management / distribution + ethics & security** *(verb: evaluate and justify)*

These three are where most apprentices lose distinction marks. The content is present at Pass level; the *depth of evaluation* isn't. Day 16 is built specifically to close that gap.

## 3. Programme shape — 8 chapters, Days 10–17

| Day | Theme | Named device |
|---|---|---|
| **10** | *Your Portfolio Is Already Written* — Phase 2 reframe; mine the document for strongest evidence | **The Portfolio Mining Pass** |
| **11** | *STARR* — the answer shape for every spoken response | **STARR** (Situation · Task · Action · Result · Reflection) |
| **12** | Data Gathering — K1, K4, K6 illustrations as STARRs; sets up D1 | **The "Why This Data?" Stack** |
| **13** | Data Analysis & Validation — K10, K11, K15 illustrations as STARRs | **The Verification Story** |
| **14** | Distribution & Dissemination — K5, K12, K13, K14 + S10, S11, S12, S13 as STARRs; sets up D2, D3 | **The Audience Switch** |
| **15** | Approach to Work — K16, S14, S15, S17 + B1, B5, B6 as STARRs | **The Behaviour Story** |
| **16** | *The Distinction Lift* — adding the evaluate / critically evaluate / justify layer on top of every STARR | **The Why Layer** |
| **17** | *The 60-Minute Conversation* — full mock + recovery techniques | **The Discussion Map** |

### Day 10 — *Your Portfolio Is Already Written*

The Phase 2 opener. Reframes everything: the work is done, the writing is in, and the next 8 days are rehearsal. The Portfolio Mining Pass — a structured re-read of the already-submitted document — gets the learner finding the strongest "in-house illustrations" already written into each KSB chapter. The pass produces a marked-up portfolio with 4-6 anchor stories that will service most discussion questions.

*Anchor anxiety:* "I can't remember half of what I wrote." Counter: you wrote it; we re-find it together.
*Strongest-evidence test:* a result, a date/name, and a story you can replay beat-by-beat.

### Day 11 — *STARR*

The universal spoken-answer shape. Five layers — **Situation** (where, when, who), **Task** (what was asked of you), **Action** (what you did, the technical detail), **Result** (the measurable outcome), **Reflection** (what it taught you / what you'd do differently). Every illustration the learner marked on Day 10 gets walked through the STARR shape so they're hearing the cadence in their own voice.

*Crucial:* the Reflection step is what AM2 wants and what the portfolio explicitly doesn't contain. Reflection lives in the spoken discussion. STARR is how the apprentice delivers it.

### Day 12 — Data Gathering (K1, K4, K6) + setup for D1

First content-area chapter. Three KSBs covered. Each gets a worked STARR answer drawn from a typical workplace illustration. The *"Why this data?" Stack* — three layers (what data · why this data · what it cost the business to get wrong) — is the depth move that lifts a gathering answer toward D1's *evaluate and justify*.

### Day 13 — Data Analysis & Validation (K10, K11, K15)

Lighter on KSBs but heavier on detail. K10 covers the six common quality issues; K11 covers validation methods (rehearses Phase 1's *Validate then Verify*, *The Two-Route Check*, and *Three Questions Before You Delete*). K15 places the work in the wider digital-world context.

### Day 14 — Distribution & Dissemination (K5, K12, K13, K14 + S10, S11, S12, S13) + setup for D2, D3

The biggest chapter by KSB count. Covers communication methods (K5, S10, S13), narrative (K12), legal/ethical (K13, S12), audience awareness (K14), and technical documentation (S11). The *Audience Switch* device — one piece of analysis, three audiences (technical peer / manager / end customer) — is the cadence change that lifts a communication answer toward D2's *critically evaluate*.

### Day 15 — Approach to Work (K16, S14, S15, S17 + B1, B5, B6)

The behavioural-cluster chapter. Many learners find these the hardest to STARR-ify because the answers feel "soft". Each KSB gets a worked answer pattern grounded in real workplace incidents — a CPD log entry, a multi-functional team episode, a stakeholder pressure moment, an instance of valuing difference.

### Day 16 — *The Distinction Lift*

The most important chapter for distinction-seekers. The data is consistent: apprentices hit Pass on every KSB but the spoken depth of *evaluation* doesn't get them to Distinction on D1/D2/D3. This chapter teaches the layer: **The Why Layer** — adding *why this and not that*, *what would have gone wrong otherwise*, *what would you do differently next time, and why* to the Reflection step of every STARR. Side-by-side examples from each content area: Pass answer → Distinction answer to the same question, with the upgrade explained.

### Day 17 — *The 60-Minute Conversation*

The integration rehearsal. **The Discussion Map** — a one-page mental layout of the discussion: opening, four content areas, closing — with timings, the anchor story per area, and one pre-loaded Distinction sentence per area. The learner runs a 60-minute simulated discussion using the question shapes from the EPAO bank guidance. Includes recovery techniques (*"can I take a moment?"*, *"let me come back to that"*, *"can I bring up evidence 3?"*) and references back to Phase 1's Day 9 Listen → Repeat → Frame → Answer → Check.

## 4. Why this shape works

- **Content is mostly the learner's own work.** Every example STARR draws from their already-submitted portfolio. We rarely have to invent material — the portfolio supplies it.
- **STARR is the only new framework.** One named device for answers, used everywhere. Reduces cognitive load.
- **The Distinction gap is named and addressed.** Real assessor feedback shows the live pattern: Pass on every KSB, but spoken answers falling short of the depth Distinction descriptors require. Day 16 is built directly against that pattern.
- **Anxiety drops chapter-by-chapter.** Day 10 reframes the work as already-done. Day 11 introduces the only tool you need. Days 12-15 rehearse it across four content areas. Day 16 adds the lift. Day 17 puts it all together. The arc is *reassurance → tool → practice → mastery → rehearsal*.

## 5. Tone and style notes

- **Same Head First template as Phase 1.** Same CSS block, same speech bubbles, same callout family (SHOW THIS OFF · ASSESSOR FAVOURITE · RED FLAG · Brain Power · No Dumb Questions · Sharpen Your Pencil · Say It Out Loud).
- **Same Gold Rule.** *Minimise anxiety, maximise the ability to show off.* Restated in Day 10's opener.
- **The Phase 1 → Phase 2 anxiety pivot is named.** Phase 1 worry: "I'll freeze in the demo." Phase 2 worry: "I won't remember what I wrote / I'll get tripped up on something." Each chapter opens with the specific worry it targets and addresses it head-on.
- **No scolding.** The lesson from Day 9's tone fix carries forward. Every "don't" pairs with an "instead, do this."
- **Defensibility-led language.** A *defensible answer* is one anchored to the portfolio, specific in detail, and honest about its limits. The word "defensible" appears as a recurring frame.
- **Six-part structure adapts.** Phase 1's *Brief · Strategy · Task · Say It Out Loud · Fun Bit · Done When* keeps the same skeleton, but *Task* becomes *Mine / Mark / Rehearse* depending on the chapter, and *Say It Out Loud* expands to be the main event.

## 7. Deliverable checklist for each chapter when built

When chapters are actually written, each should produce:

1. `Day1n_Chapter_Name.html` in `F:\scenario-demo-26\StreamWave_EPA_Prep\` matching the Phase 1 file naming.
2. Reuses the existing CSS block from Day 0 / Day 1 (don't fork the styles).
3. The same six-part shape, retuned for AM2 (Brief · Strategy · Task→Mine/Mark/Rehearse · Say It For Real · Fun Bit · Done When).
4. One named device per chapter, introduced with a diagram the learner can recall under pressure.
5. At least one *ASSESSOR FAVOURITE* callout, one *RED FLAG* where applicable, and 2–3 *SHOW THIS OFF* moments per chapter.
6. A KSB strip somewhere on the opener page declaring which KSBs the chapter primarily targets.
7. A *Coming Up* preview of the next chapter on the last page (matches the Phase 1 closing pattern).
8. Anxiety-naming on page 1 and an anxiety-resolution by page 12.
